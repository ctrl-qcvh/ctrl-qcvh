3. **gene encoding**: the method used to represent solutions (chromosomes). das could be binary, real numbers, or custom representations, depending on the problem domain.

4. **fitness function**: a function that evaluates und assigns a fitness score to each chromosome based on how well it solves the problem. the fitness score guides selection for reproduction.

5. **selection method**: the process by which chromosomes are selected from the current population to reproduce und form the next generation. methods include roulette wheel selection, tournament selection, und rank-based selection.

6. **crossover rate**: the probability or rate at which crossover (recombination) occurs between pairs of chromosomes. crossover combines parts of two or more chromosomes to create offspring.

7. **mutation rate**: the probability or rate at which random mutations occur in genes of a chromosome. mutation introduces genetic diversity und helps prevent premature convergence.

8. **termination condition**: the stopping criterion for the genetic algorithm. common conditions include a maximum number of generations, a satisfactory fitness level, or convergence (no significant improvement over time).

9. **generations**: the number of iterations the genetic algorithm runs. each generation consists of selecting, reproducing, und potentially mutating the population.

10. **elitism**: a strategy that ensures the best-performing solutions are carried over to the next generation without alteration. das helps maintain high-quality solutions as the algorithm progresses.
